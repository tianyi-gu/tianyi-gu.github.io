On Strings and Petals and Other Tensions I've Tried to Hold

Tianyi Gu
5.26.2025
ENG519: Hybrid Forms

If Marionette in Motion had a mouth, I think it would whisper rather than speak. It would release its mutterings into the wind, its whimsically metaphoric musings, maybe sneeze occasionally—if sculptures can sneeze (I rather like to think they can).

~

The foothills of Colorado's famed Rocky Mountains are filled with wind. As the gusts came streaking down the hills and brushing past treetops, they flew straight through my front door and filled my childhood with movement too.

Movement in the way laundry billowed on the backyard clothesline.

Movement in the newspaper pages curling mid-sentence.

Movement in the frighteningly electric energy that can fill a house with a mother who lets music rock the house, a sister who finds joy in slamming every door twice just to make sure it's closed, a father who paces through every hallway, and a son who feels far too much far too often.

Marionette in Motion (see exhibit description) is meant to pause. It is the instant before a breath enters the lung. It is my way of reclaiming the moments passed in furious blurs. Through the inclusion of motorized elements in each of the sculptures, there holds a promise for movement. It is potential not yet turned into action.

~

My parents are both engineers. In the same way genes are inherited from one generation to the next, oftentimes too are disciplines and interests. It is inevitable that the environment that one is raised and nurtured in plays quite the formative role in how a person turns out. And yet, this is only somewhat true. While my childhood was filled with afternoons spent on math problems and dinner-table conversations on logic puzzles, it is also unavoidable that a person's nature will draw them towards that which is inexplicable. While art and writing had never been in the cards for me, they showed up anyways, manifesting themselves in the silent appreciation after reading a poem or the underlying joy seeing an intriguing piece of art.

In the same way, while I am unversed in the creation of art, I let myself and my imagination run wild in the creation of my exhibits. Quite intentionally, I created the exhibit with a blending of mediums (robotics & newspaper) as a mirror for the way each of us are the intersection of genres/ideas. In each of my creations, I prioritized leaving room for what I loved most about art, the ability for it to reflect one's own interpretation.

fly away: reading the whisper of a caged bird and replying in verse

clothesline: the hanging and remembrance of secrets, memories, and people

rose garden: folding your own rose to finish filling a garden of flowers

~

In reflection, exhibit is perhaps the wrong word for what I have created. An exhibit ("public display"), seems to indicate a sort of barrier between the viewer and what is being viewed. It implies a unidirectional relationship. From this term, whether it be Tommy Kha's Chain Letter Project or Amy Krouse Rosenthal's experiments in Encyclopedia of an Ordinary Life, what I have enjoyed the most are those pieces of art which we can feel in conversation with.

I have not set out to create an exhibit. Rather a place to whisper to a sculpture and hear it whisper back (see fly away). A place to hang your own memories next to mine and let them mingle in the breeze as they dangle from the clothesline (see clothesline). A space where you are not just a viewer but a co-creator—an artist yourself—one petal, one rosebud at a time (see rose garden). Maybe communion is a better word.

~

Perhaps it is better that my vision of a kinetic sculpture wasn't fully realized. Reflected in those sculptures' inability to move one finds the imperfection and potential within all of us. This is something tender. It is still unfolding. If it is an invitation then that is enough.

~

Artist Bio

Tianyi Evans Gu (b. 2006-) is a writer-artist-tinkerer-rememberer. As a graduating Senior from Phillips Academy, he dabbles across and in between disciplines. Tianyi is especially interested in capturing those moments that seem so difficult to capture, ideas like motion, stillness, absence, and memory. When he's not spending time writing and crafting things, he can be found daydreaming about shooting stars, playing tennis and poker with friends, or in discovery of his inner child. Tianyi believes art should be a mirror that fogs when you breathe near it.
